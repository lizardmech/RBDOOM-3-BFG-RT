Glass Reflection Correction Lane
================================

Status: NOT STARTED. This document supersedes all previous glass shading
guidance for the reflection/specular problem. Read section 1 before touching
any file. Revision 2: corrected to reuse the renderer's EXISTING traced-
reflection shading infrastructure instead of inventing new shading.

Goal: glass currently renders as a transparent surface with a slight tint.
Reflections and light highlights are missing. This lane adds them.

1. Why every previous attempt failed (read this, it saves you hours)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Four facts. Do not re-investigate them; they have been verified against this
codebase and the RTX Remix reference.

FACT 1: No reflected radiance ever reaches the GLASS lane.
    The only "reflection" in the glass compose path is a fabricated gray
    color:

        pathtrace_clean_rtxdi_di_glass.hlsli, line ~141:
        reflectedProxy = max(baseColor.rgb, float3(0.45, 0.48, 0.52))

    That is not scene light. It is a constant. No ray is traced in the
    reflected direction anywhere in the glass pipeline, and no texture the
    glass compose reads stores reflected light. Tuning reflectionBoost,
    transmissionFloor, or any compose math can only rescale this gray
    constant. That is the dead end previous workers were in.

FACT 2: RTXDI / light sampling can NEVER produce specular on this glass.
    The glass BSDF is a Dirac delta (perfectly smooth mirror lobe; the file
    header of pathtrace_clean_rtxdi_di_glass_math.hlsli says so: "dirac thin
    glass, no roughness"). A delta lobe evaluates to zero for every light
    sample direction, so NEE/RTXDI shading of the glass surface itself is
    mathematically zero. Any task of the form "make the DI pass add specular
    to glass pixels" is unsatisfiable. Do not attempt it. Highlights on
    glass must come from a traced mirror ray hitting bright things.

FACT 3: The renderer ALREADY traces and shades reflections - for opaque
glossy surfaces. Glass is gated out of both existing paths.

    Path 1 (production, clean lane): the clean ReSTIR GI unit
    (neo/shaders/builtin/pathtracing/remix_restir_gi/
    pathtrace_clean_restir_gi.rt.hlsl) samples specular bounce directions
    for glossy opaque surfaces (CleanGiSampleSpecularProducerDirection,
    CleanGiSampleGlossySecondRayDirection) and shades secondary hits with
    real direct lighting (CleanGiShadeDirectVertex /
    CleanGiShadeDirectVertexDefaultOneSample: emissive + one light sample
    drawn from the analytic + emissive-triangle domains, with visibility
    trace and BRDF eval). This is why glossy objects reflect correctly in
    the current renderer.

    Path 2 (legacy/standalone lane): pathtrace_smoke.rt.hlsl line ~3466,
    EvaluateRestirPTTracedReflectionFromSurface, dispatched as the
    standalone reflection producer (pathtrace_restir_reflection_producer
    .rt.hlsl) writing RestirPTReflectionOutput (u47). Full mirror trace +
    NEE shading.

    Why glass gets neither:
    - Both paths gate on opaque surfaces (Path 2 explicitly:
      RAB_SurfaceSupportsOpaqueDiffuseBrdf; glass records fail it).
    - By the time the GI lane runs on a glass pixel, the transmission PSR
      phase has already REPLACED the primary-surface record with the
      surface BEHIND the glass. The GI shades that backdrop surface; the
      glass mirror lobe no longer exists anywhere it can see.

FACT 4: Do not consume RestirPTReflectionOutput (u47) from the glass lane.
    It is not dispatched in the clean view 16 route, and its output is
    preview-TONEMAPPED (RestirPTGiToneMapPreview), not linear radiance.
    Wiring it into the clean compose gives wrong energy and a dependency on
    a lane that does not run. The thing to reuse is the SHADING PATTERN
    from the clean GI lane, not the legacy texture.

What IS already correct and must be reused, not rewritten:

    - pathtrace_clean_rtxdi_di_glass_math.hlsli: complete thin-glass
      physics - Fresnel with TIR, Beer-Lambert, thin-film interreflection
      series for BOTH reflection and transmission throughput. The payload
      struct already carries payload.reflection (float3 reflection
      throughput). No changes needed.
    - The transmission producer already knows how to trace a continuation
      ray and build a shaded-able surface from the hit
      (PathTraceCleanRtxdiDiTraceTransmissionHit +
      PathTraceCleanRtxdiDiBuildResolvedSurfaceFromTraceHit).
    - The clean GI lane already knows how to shade an arbitrary hit point
      with direct light (CleanGiShadeDirectVertexDefaultOneSample and its
      helper chain). This is the exemplar for the new reflection shading.
    - The light buffers the shading needs (SmokeEmissiveTriangles t16,
      DoomAnalyticLights t27, plus CleanRtxdiDiAnalyticLightCount /
      CleanRtxdiDiCurrentEmissiveTriangleCount constants) are declared in
      pathtrace_clean_rtxdi_di_shared.hlsli, which the transmission
      producer ALREADY includes. The data is in scope; only the sampling/
      evaluation helper functions need to be made available (task B6b).

Missing pieces, and the ONLY missing pieces: a mirror ray from the glass
surface, a place to store the shaded result, and a compose term that adds
it. That is what this lane builds.

3. OPTION B: deterministic reflection sidecar (DO THIS FIRST)
-------------------------------------------------------------

Design in one paragraph (context only; the tasks below are the contract):
The producer PSR phase is the only pass that still has the glass surface in
hand. Add one mirror-reflection ray there. Shade the hit the same way the
clean GI lane already shades its secondary hits: hit emissive plus one
direct light sample from the analytic + emissive-triangle domains. The
producer unit already includes the light buffers via
pathtrace_clean_rtxdi_di_shared.hlsli, so only the evaluation helpers need
to be brought in. Premultiply by the reflection throughput glass_math
already computes, store in a NEW second sidecar texture, and have the
compose pass ADD it. Deterministic per-pixel work (one ray + one shadow
ray), no reservoir changes.

New data flow:

    producer: also traces reflect(-view, normal) ray, shades hit
              (emissive + 1 light sample),
              writes reflection sidecar u90 = radiance * payload.reflection
        -> DI / GI unchanged
        -> compose: out = shadedBackdrop * transmissionThroughput
                        + reflectionSidecar.rgb        (already premultiplied)

5. OPTION A: upgrade to Remix-style stochastic reflection PSR (LATER)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Do not start until Option B has shipped and been visually accepted. Option B
stays as the fallback path behind a cvar. This section is a map, not a task
list; when the time comes, a supervisor breaks it into atomic tasks the same
way section 3 is written.

Why bother after Option B works: Option B shades reflection hits with ONE
light sample and no GI. Option A instead publishes the reflected-world
surface into the primary-surface record (exactly like the transmission PSR
does for the transmitted world), so the FULL existing pipeline - RTXDI DI
with temporal/spatial reuse, the clean GI lane including its glossy
specular handling, and DLSS-RR - shades the reflection natively. Since the
renderer already shades everything in the primary record correctly
(FACT 3), reflections inherit all of it for free. This is exactly the RTX
Remix architecture.

Reference implementation to mirror (read before designing anything):
    E:/prog/references/dxvk-remix-git/src/dxvk/shaders/rtx/algorithm/
    geometry_resolver.slangh, from the "samplePSR" block (~line 1900):
    - polymorphicSurfaceMaterialInteractionCalcPSRSample produces BOTH a
      reflection sample and a transmission sample plus
      reflectionSelectedIntegrationSurface and selectedIntegrationSurfacePdf.
    - The chosen lane's throughput is accumulated as attenuation and stored;
      both PSR data buffers exist (storeReflectionPSRData /
      storeTransmissionPSRData).
    Selection PDF: Fresnel-weighted. For our thin dirac glass the weights
    are luminance(payload.reflection) vs luminance(payload.transmission),
    normalized. Divide the lane's contribution by its selection probability
    at compose.

Incremental migration steps (each gated by a debug view):

    A1. Selection: in the producer PSR phase, draw one RNG sample, pick
        reflection with probability pR = luminance(reflection) /
        (luminance(reflection) + luminance(transmission)). Publish the
        reflected hit surface instead of the transmitted one on
        reflection-selected pixels. Store in the sidecar: which lane was
        selected, the lane throughput, and pR.
        Debug view: selected-lane mask (red = reflection, green =
        transmission).
    A2. Compose: for reflection-selected pixels the shaded output IS the
        reflected world; compose = shaded * reflectionThroughput / pR, plus
        the transmission tint using Option B's machinery as the cross-lane
        estimate (and vice versa for transmission-selected pixels, keeping
        Option B's traced reflection term). Both terms present every frame,
        bounded noise.
    A3. Motion vectors: reflected surfaces need virtual positions or the
        temporal DI pass and DLSS-RR will reject/ghost them. Remix
        accumulates a rotation quaternion through PSR (accumulatedRotation)
        and still documents it as imperfect (TODO REMIX-3500). Start by
        writing the VIRTUAL hit position (mirror-world position behind the
        pane) into the primary-surface record's position field - that is
        what Remix's "virtual hit distance" comments describe
        (geometry_resolver.slangh ~lines 250 and 483). Debug view: temporal
        reprojection error on glass (helpers in
        pathtrace_clean_rtxdi_di_rr_debug.hlsli already visualize this).
    A4. Only if A1-A3 hold up: let temporal DI history survive on
        reflection-selected pixels (check what the temporal pass keys
        history on - if world position, A3's virtual position already
        handles it).

Known hazards (state them in every Option A task so workers do not "fix"
them ad hoc):
    - Noise: lane selection is per-pixel stochastic; DLSS-RR is expected to
      resolve it (Remix ships exactly this). Do not add blur passes.
    - TIR pixels: payload.reflection ~ 1, transmission ~ 0 -> pR ~ 1;
      selection degenerates gracefully, no special case needed.
    - Do not attempt reflection AND transmission full shading per pixel
      (two DI invocations); different and much more expensive design, out
      of scope.


6. Forbidden approaches (repeat offenders)
------------------------------------------

    - Adding specular response to RTXDI/NEE evaluation for glass pixels.
      (Zero by construction on a delta lobe. See FACT 2.)
    - Tuning reflectionBoost / transmissionFloor / compose lerps to try to
      make reflections appear. (There is no reflected radiance to boost
      until Task B6 exists.)
    - Consuming RestirPTReflectionOutput (u47) or flipping the
      RAB_SurfaceSupportsOpaqueDiffuseBrdf gate in the legacy smoke
      reflection producer. (Wrong lane, not dispatched in view 16, output
      is preview-tonemapped, and cross-lane branches violate the
      modular-shaders primary rule. Reuse the PATTERN, not the pass.)
    - Editing the GI raygen (pathtrace_clean_restir_gi.rt.hlsl) or the DI
      sentinel to special-case glass. The only sanctioned sentinel change
      is MOVING existing light-build helpers into a shared include (B6b),
      byte-identical.
    - Screen-space reflection via the dead offset helpers in glass_math.
      (They approximate offsets, not radiance; deleted in B10.)
    - Giving the glass roughness so light sampling works. (Changes the
      material model; out of scope.)
    - Touching reservoir buffers, temporal/spatial DI passes, or reservoir
      logic anywhere. Option B is producer + compose + C++ plumbing only.
