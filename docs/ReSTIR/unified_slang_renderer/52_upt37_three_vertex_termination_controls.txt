UPT-37: runtime roulette and initial-only throughput termination
================================================================

Status
------

Implemented as runtime controls on the default-off UPT-35/36 specialization.
Build and runtime acceptance passed. Keep the specialization optional: the
extra bounce is difficult to justify for Doom 3 content, but the completed
D0/T0 transport is retained for renderer ports whose materials, scale or
lighting make the additional depth worthwhile.

Controls
--------

    r_pathTracingUnifiedPtThreeVertexContinueProbability
        default 0.8, clamped [0,1]

    r_pathTracingUnifiedPtThreeVertexMinimumPathThroughput
        default 0.0, clamped [0,1]

The first value is the x2->x3 Russian-roulette survival probability q. q=0
disables that continuation without emitting a ray; lower positive values retain
unbiased 1/q compensation but increase variance.

The second value follows RTXDI FullSample `minimumPathThroughput`. It is a
hard, uncompensated and therefore biased kill threshold. The test is:

    dot(T, T) < minimumPathThroughput * minimumPathThroughput

where T is the accumulated RGB f*NoL/pdf throughput after sampling the x2 BSDF
but before the current roulette compensation. This is squared RGB L2 length,
not luminance or max component, and avoids a square root.

Ordering and replay contract
----------------------------

D0 order is now:

    sample x2 BSDF
    form uncompensated accumulated throughput
    apply minimum-throughput kill
    draw roulette survival with runtime q
    apply 1/q throughput and q path-PDF factors
    emit x2->x3 ray

The cutoff creates no candidate and no ray. It has no compensation.

T0 deliberately does not apply the cutoff. A selected D0 sample already proved
that the initial path survived it; repeating a receiver-dependent cutoff during
hybrid-shift replay would desynchronize the mapping. T0 instead validates and
uses the q stored in proposalPdf and accumulatedRouletteProbability. It never
reads the live q cvar for a history sample.

ABI and invalidation
--------------------

No push-constant size is added. The two binary32 values travel through the
existing reservedControl0/1 uint words at offsets 200 and 204, preserving the
accepted 208-byte reflected ABI. Other D0 artifacts ignore those words.

The clamped bit patterns of q and the threshold participate in the UPT content
generation fingerprint only while three-vertex transport is effective.
Changing either cvar therefore rejects incompatible history without clearing a
full buffer. New D0 samples publish the new q and T0 replays that stored value.

Test presets
------------

    exec uptb3
        baseline: q=0.8, threshold=0.0, temporal on, spatial off

    exec uptb4
        aggressive trial: q=0.25, threshold=0.10, temporal on, spatial off

The aggressive preset is intentionally not a proposed production default. It
exists to measure ray-count/performance sensitivity and visible variance/bias.
Compare fixed camera positions after history warm-up and inspect dark indirect
regions, small emitters, motion, energy stability and temporal convergence.

Runtime result
--------------

The deployed q=0.8/threshold=0 baseline and q=0.25/threshold=0.10 aggressive
configuration both ran correctly. No replay corruption, ghost emitters,
geometry loss, motion poisoning or runaway energy was reported. The feature is
therefore accepted as an optional three-vertex path.

Do not infer that the aggressive preset is a new default. For Doom 3 the added
bounce remains expensive relative to its visible benefit. Preserve the
one-continuation `uptf2` lane as the production-shaped choice, and use the two
termination controls to tune the optional deeper path for future renderer
ports.

Offline result
--------------

Both focused Vulkan shader targets compile, validate and pass their structural
verifiers. D0 retains 25 bindings, a 208-byte push constant, three static
RayQuery sites and zero TraceRay sites. T0 retains 30 bindings, six static
RayQuery sites and zero TraceRay sites; its verifier confirms stored-q replay
and the absence of a throughput-cutoff route.
